Use your ferrite estimate and the density data to calculate its mass fraction, then invert the lever rule at A1. What is the approximate original carbon content?

Enter wt% C to two decimal places. Your calculation is checked against your ferrite estimate, with a tolerance of ±0.01 wt% C.

Worked solution using an estimate of 15%

  1. The pale-blue regions between pearlite colonies occupy roughly 15% of the image area. Taking area fraction ≈ volume fraction gives Vαpro ≈ 0.15 and VP ≈ 0.85.
  2. Pearlite contains WFe₃CP = (0.760 − 0.022)/(6.67 − 0.022) = 0.111 and WαP = 0.889.
  3. ρP = [0.889/7.87 + 0.111/7.68]−1 = 7.85 g cm−3.
  4. Wαpro = (0.15 × 7.87)/[(0.15 × 7.87) + (0.85 × 7.85)] ≈ 0.1503, or about 15.0 mass%.
  5. C0 = Ce − Wαpro(Ce − Cα) = 0.760 − 0.1503(0.760 − 0.022) ≈ 0.649 wt% C ≈ 0.65 wt% C.

The 15% reference is an approximate visual estimate. Your carbon-content answer is checked using your own accepted ferrite estimate. This introductory treatment neglects small redistribution effects below A1.

Density sources and scope

The ferrite value uses the rounded room-temperature density of pure iron reported by the National Bureau of Standards/NIST. The cementite value is rounded from the recommended ambient-condition value compiled in the ISIJ International cementite review. The austenite context value is estimated from the temperature- and carbon-dependent lattice-parameter relation reported by Onink and co-workers; it is not used in the answer.
