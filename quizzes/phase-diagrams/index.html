One-question challenge

Estimate the original carbon content

A slowly cooled hypoeutectoid steel has the final microstructure shown. For this exercise, a separate quantitative image analysis reports that proeutectoid α-ferrite occupies 49.0% of a representative section area; the remainder is pearlite.

Given estimate 49.0 area% proeutectoid α Take area fraction ≈ volume fraction.

Use the density data to convert this to a mass fraction, then invert the lever rule at A1. What is the approximate original steel composition?

Enter the composition as a percentage, for example 0.40 rather than 0.004 or 40.

Worked solution

  1. Pearlite contains WFe₃CP = (0.760 − 0.022)/(6.67 − 0.022) = 0.111 and WαP = 0.889.
  2. ρP = [0.889/7.87 + 0.111/7.68]−1 = 7.85 g cm−3.
  3. Wαpro = (0.490 × 7.87)/[(0.490 × 7.87) + (0.510 × 7.85)] = 0.491.
  4. C0 = Ce − Wαpro(Ce − Cα) = 0.760 − 0.491(0.760 − 0.022) = 0.398 wt% C ≈ 0.40 wt% C.

The stated 49.0% is supplied exercise data, not a measurement taken from the synthetic image. The usual introductory treatment also neglects small redistribution effects below A1.

Density sources and scope

The ferrite value uses the rounded room-temperature density of pure iron reported by the National Bureau of Standards/NIST. The cementite value is rounded from the recommended ambient-condition value compiled in the ISIJ International cementite review. The austenite context value is estimated from the temperature- and carbon-dependent lattice-parameter relation reported by Onink and co-workers; it is not used in the answer.
