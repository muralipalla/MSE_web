Crystallography quiz

Indices, planes, and planar density

Read intercepts, convert Miller–Bravais notation, and count atom centres on FCC, BCC, and HCP planes. Numerical answers within 1.5% are accepted.

Conventions

Use these definitions throughout

  • Cubic questions use the conventional cell parameter a.
  • HCP uses basal parameter a, height c, and a two-atom basis.
  • Planar density is the number of atom centres in the shown two-dimensional repeat area.
  • For four-index hexagonal planes, h + k + i = 0.
  • Input format: write a negative index as bar(1) for 1minus 1. In a numerical box, enter -1.

Part A

Miller indices in cubic crystals

1. Intercepts to indices

A plane intercepts x at a, is parallel to y, and intercepts z at a/2. What are its Miller indices?

Choose the Miller indices

Normalize the intercepts as (1, ∞, 1/2), take reciprocals, and clear fractions: (102).

2. Negative intercept

A plane intercepts x at a, y at −a/2, and is parallel to z. Choose its indices.

Choose the Miller indices

The reciprocal intercepts are 1, −2, and 0, so the plane is (12minus 20).

3. Cubic plane spacing

For a cubic crystal, calculate d221/a.

dhkl = a/√(h2+k2+l2), so d221/a = 1/√9 = 0.3333.

Part B

Miller–Bravais notation

4. Complete the plane indices

Complete the missing index in (2 1minus 1 ? 0).

For a four-index hexagonal plane, i = −(h+k) = −(2−1) = −1. The plane is (2 1minus 1 1minus 1 0).

5. Convert a hexagonal plane

Convert the three-index hexagonal plane (101) to four-index Miller–Bravais notation.

Choose the four-index plane

For planes, (H K L) → (H K −H−K L). Thus (101) becomes (101minus 11).

6. Convert a hexagonal direction

Convert the three-index direction [101] to the smallest integer four-index direction.

Choose the four-index direction

Direction conversion gives [2/3, −1/3, −1/3, 1]. Multiply by 3 to obtain [21minus 11minus 13]. Plane and direction conversions are different.

Part C

Planar density

Use a = 0.400 nm for FCC and BCC. For HCP use a = 0.300 nm and c = 0.490 nm. Give answers in atoms nm−2.

7. FCC (100)

Area = a2. Four corners contribute 1 atom and the face centre contributes 1: 2/a2 = 12.500.

8. FCC (110)

Area = √2a2. Four corners and two edge-centred atoms contribute 2 effective atoms: √2/a2 = 8.839.

9. FCC (111)

The triangular patch has area √3a2/2. Three vertices contribute 1/2 atom and three edge centres contribute 3/2, so 4/(√3a2) = 14.434.

10. BCC (100)

The square contains four corner quarters = 1 effective atom. Therefore 1/a2 = 6.250.

11. BCC (110)

Area = √2a2. Corner fractions contribute 1 atom and the body centre contributes 1: √2/a2 = 8.839.

12. BCC (111)

The triangular patch area is √3a2/2; three vertex sixths total 1/2 atom. Thus 1/(√3a2) = 3.608.

13. HCP (0001)

A primitive basal rhombus has area √3a2/2 and one atom: 2/(√3a2) = 12.830.

14. HCP (101minus 10)

The shown prism repeat rectangle has area ac and one effective atom: 1/(ac) = 6.803.

15. HCP (112minus 20)

The repeat rectangle has area √3ac and contains 2 effective atoms: 2/(√3ac) = 7.855.
