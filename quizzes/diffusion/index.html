Diffusion quiz

From random walks to Fick’s laws

Connect atom-scale motion to continuum transport. Work in SI units unless a question specifies otherwise; numerical answers within the stated tolerance are accepted.

Reference

Relations used in this quiz

Fick I: J = −D ∂C/∂x

Fick II: ∂C/∂t = D ∂2C/∂x2

Random walk: ⟨x2⟩ = 2Dt in 1D and ⟨r2⟩ = 4Dt in 2D

Arrhenius: D = D0 exp[−Q/(RT)]

Part A

Fick’s laws and flux

1. Direction of diffusion

At a point in a solid, D is positive and dC/dx > 0. In which direction is the diffusive flux?

Choose the direction of the diffusive flux

Fick’s first law is J = −D dC/dx. A positive concentration gradient therefore gives a flux in the negative x direction, toward lower concentration.

2. Calculate a steady flux

D = 1.5 × 10−10 m2/s. Concentration falls from 2.4 × 104 to 0.8 × 104 mol/m3 over 2.0 mm. Find J, taking the positive direction toward the lower-concentration side.

dC/dx = (−1.6 × 104)/(2.0 × 10−3) = −8.0 × 106 mol m−4. Thus J = 1.2 × 10−3 mol m−2 s−1 = 1.20 mmol m−2 s−1.

3. Recognise a steady profile

For constant D, no sources, and fixed boundary concentrations, what does Fick’s second law predict for an initially linear concentration profile?

Choose what happens to the linear concentration profile

Fick’s second law gives ∂C/∂t = D∂2C/∂x2. A linear profile has zero curvature, so ∂C/∂t = 0 and it remains steady.

Part B

Random walks and continuum profiles

4. Two-dimensional diffusion distance

For isotropic 2D diffusion, D = 2.0 × 10−12 m2/s and t = 250 s. Find the root-mean-square radial displacement.

In 2D, ⟨r2⟩ = 4Dt. Therefore rrms = √(4Dt) = √[4(2.0 × 10−12)(250)] = 44.72 µm.

5. Time to diffuse

How long does a species with D = 3.0 × 10−11 m2/s take to reach a 1D rms displacement of 12 µm?

In 1D, ⟨x2⟩ = 2Dt. Thus t = xrms2/(2D) = (12 × 10−6)2/[2(3.0 × 10−11)] = 2.40 s.

6. Step-profile solution

Initially, the fraction of B is 0 for x < 0 and 1 for x > 0. For constant D, what is the fraction of B at x = 2√(Dt)? Give your answer as a percentage.

The step solution is CB = ½[1 + erf(x/2√(Dt))]. Here the error-function argument is 1, so CB = ½[1 + 0.84270] = 0.92135, or 92.14%.

Part C

Numerical methods and temperature

7. Explicit-scheme stability

A 1D forward-time, centred-space solver uses Δx = 1.0 µm and D = 1.0 × 10−12 m2/s. Find the largest stable time step.

For the 1D explicit scheme, DΔt/Δx2 ≤ ½. Therefore Δtmax = Δx2/(2D) = (10−6)2/(2 × 10−12) = 0.50 s.

8. Arrhenius temperature dependence

For an activation energy Q = 120 kJ/mol, by what factor does D increase when temperature rises from 800 K to 1000 K? Assume the same D0 and use R = 8.314 J mol−1 K−1.

D2/D1 = exp{−(Q/R)[1/T2 − 1/T1]} = exp{−(120000/8.314)(1/1000 − 1/800)} = 36.91.
