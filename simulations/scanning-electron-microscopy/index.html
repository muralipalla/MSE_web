Interpretation safeguards

Read the diagram as a signal map

Secondary-electron origins

SE1 is generated directly by the primary beam near the impact point. SE2 is generated when a returning BSE excites a secondary electron at a laterally displaced surface location, broadening the effective source.

Energy is not one number

SE is conventionally below 50 eV. BSE has a broad distribution from above that boundary toward E0; it should not be treated as a single fixed-energy beam.

Atomic relaxation

Characteristic X-ray emission and Auger-electron emission are competing relaxation routes after an inner-shell vacancy. They are not compulsory paired products of one event.

What is outside this bulk view?

SE3 can arise when BSE strikes chamber hardware. Transmitted electrons require a thin electron-transparent specimen, while EBSD uses an angle-resolved subset of BSE.
