5

Post-test check

Interpret the experiment

Complete one test to unlock four numerical questions and the calculation CSV used to check them.

1. Calculate Young’s modulus from the initial linear portion of the data.

Use E = Δσ / Δε. Enter the result in GPa.

2. Determine the ultimate tensile strength from the maximum engineering stress.

Find the greatest stress value in your recorded data or curve.

3. Calculate the approximate modulus of resilience.

Integrate stress–strain data up to the elastic limit. For a linear elastic region, Ur = σ2 / (2E).

4. Estimate the tensile toughness from the complete engineering stress–strain data.

Use the trapezoidal rule to find the total area under the curve up to fracture.

i

Scientific basis

Geometry, data, and limits

The specimen outlines preserve the main proportions of common test specimens. The curves join published endpoint properties with clearly idealized interpolation so that the deformation stages can be explored. They are educational examples, not material certificates or design data.

Standards and specimen proportions
  • Metals use the 100 mm overall, 25 mm gauge, 6 mm reduced-width subsize outline summarized for ASTM E8/E8M-25 and reproduced by ZwickRoell; 3 mm thickness is an illustrative choice.
  • Polycarbonate uses ASTM D638 Type I proportions: 165 mm overall, 50 mm gauge, 13 mm narrow width, and 3.2 mm illustrative thickness. See ASTM D638-22 and Instron’s dimension summary.
  • Alumina uses a C1273-inspired cylindrical button-head outline, 160 mm overall with a 35 mm gauge and 6.35 mm gauge diameter. ASTM C1273-18(2024) permits several suggested ceramic geometries and emphasizes alignment and flaw statistics; this drawing is not a compliance specimen.
Representative material property sources
  • A36 steel: 250 MPa minimum yield, 400–550 MPa tensile range, and minimum elongation from SSAB ASTM A36; 200 GPa modulus from the AISC Specification.
  • Annealed C11000 copper: modulus, 0.5%-extension yield, tensile strength, and elongation from the Copper Development Association.
  • Heat-treated M2 HSS: strength and elongation from Sandvik Osprey M2; 219 GPa modulus from the Böhler S600 datasheet.
  • AD-995 alumina: 262 MPa representative direct tensile strength from CoorsTek ceramic sensor data and 370–380 GPa modulus from CoorsTek advanced alumina data.
  • Makrolon 2405 polycarbonate: 2.4 GPa modulus, 65 MPa yield at 6%, and 125% strain at break from the Covestro-authored datasheet.

Specimen outlines are ASTM-proportioned illustrations only. Consult the current purchased standard and applicable product specification for tolerances, preparation, conditioning, alignment, strain measurement, safety, and rate. Real polymers and ceramics can be strongly rate- and environment-sensitive; this simulation changes elapsed time, not the selected representative curve.
