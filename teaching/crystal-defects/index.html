Crystal Defects

A perfect lattice is a useful reference, but real materials contain departures from perfect order. Those departures enable diffusion, control ionic transport, impede dislocations, and divide a solid into grains.

Defects are not simply mistakes: engineers create, remove, and arrange them to tune properties.

A hierarchy of imperfect order

Classify a defect by its dimensional extent

0D

Point

Vacancies, solutes, and interstitials disturb a few atomic sites.

1D

Line

Dislocations carry lattice slip through a crystal.

2D

Planar

Grain boundaries, twins, stacking faults, and surfaces interrupt order across an area.

3D

Volume

Pores, inclusions, and second-phase particles occupy a finite volume.

Follow a line defect

Edge and screw dislocations in three dimensions

An edge dislocation is the line where an extra half-plane terminates; for a pure edge dislocation b ⊥ ξ. A screw dislocation has a helicoidal displacement field; for a pure screw dislocation b ∥ ξ. Most real dislocation lines have mixed character.

From imperfection to performance

What should you carry forward?

  • Vacancies and interstitials enable diffusion but also distort the surrounding lattice.
  • Void size is geometric; actual site occupancy is controlled by chemistry and energy.
  • Ionic defect reactions must respect site balance, composition, and charge neutrality.
  • Edge and screw dislocations differ in the relationship between the Burgers vector and the dislocation line.
  • Grain boundaries can accelerate diffusion and obstruct dislocation motion; grain size therefore changes strength and transport.
